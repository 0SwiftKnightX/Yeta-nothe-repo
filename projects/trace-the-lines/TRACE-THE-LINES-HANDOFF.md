## 2. Creative brief

Create an anime-inspired psychological mystery that grows from uncertainty into confrontation and then release. The story follows a solitary protagonist through a reflective corridor/maze, connected by a blue thread. A key, map, torn-page book, and mirror recur as clues. The reflection gradually behaves independently; the corridor fractures into parallel versions of itself. At the end, the protagonist steps out of the loop.

### Visual identity
- Vertical 9:16 composition for the first delivery.
- Cinematic anime background art, expressive but restrained character acting.
- Charcoal/black environments, cyan-blue thread and lighting, restrained magenta highlights.
- Wet reflective floors, glass, impossible corridors, floating pages, map-like linework, controlled glitch/fracture effects.
- One consistent protagonist across shots: silver/ash hair, blue-gray eyes, dark hoodie/clothing, blue thread tied around wrist. This is a proposed design to be approved, not an established character from the original track.
- Recurring symbol: blue thread. Its meaning changes from a tether to a clue and finally a connection the character can choose to follow or leave.
- Avoid generic filler, random spectacle, and visual effects that do not relate to lyrics or musical changes.
- Do not generate replacement lyrics, subtitles, or text inside video-generation tools. Add any verified lyric typography during editing if needed.

## 3. Lyric and energy analysis — confidence notes

Working motifs identified from visible lyric cards:
- Identity and recognition: name, map, key, a face/reflection.
- Memory and familiarity: “Something I knew / Before I knew” (verify against the source before final timing).
- A voice/feedback questioning who is speaking.
- Repetition, wrong turns, doors, and a corridor that returns.
- An emphatic section associated with “NO STRAIGHT LINES, NO GUARANTEED EXIT” in prior discussion; verify exact text/audio placement.
- A possible final resolution around “I’m here”; verify exact lyric and placement.

A prior rough audio-signal measurement suggested an opening quieter than later sections and higher average signal energy near the end. This is not a substitute for listening analysis: RMS energy is not emotional intensity, and an estimated tempo (~99 BPM) must be independently verified before editing to beat.

**Required before locking the storyboard:** verify lyrics against the source MP4 and listen/check waveform transitions. Preserve the original audio stream unchanged in the final edit.

## 6. Workflow: source to final export

1. **Inventory and preserve**
   - Identify the Library MP4 and any storyboard images already generated.
   - Keep an untouched copy of the source.
   - Record file size, duration, dimensions, frame rate, audio codec, sample rate, and channel count when available.
2. **Create a verified lyric map**
   - Read each on-screen lyric card and cross-check with listening.
   - Record exact start/end time for each line; flag uncertain words rather than guessing.
   - Mark vocal entries, instrumental sections, energy changes, accents, and transitions.
3. **Lock creative direction**
   - Approve protagonist, thread, palette, corridor, mirror, book, key, map, and ending.
   - Build reference frames for character front/side/back and key environments.
4. **Test the opening before scaling**
   - Generate a 4–6 second silent motion test only after confirming cost and obtaining approval.
   - Evaluate identity consistency, motion quality, visual clarity, and fit to the source moment.
   - Do not spend remaining credits on bulk generations until the test passes.
5. **Generate shot batches**
   - Create shots by narrative continuity, not as independent random prompts.
   - Reuse approved reference images and exact character description.
   - Generate silent video; add original audio only during edit.
6. **Assemble**
   - Create a vertical 9:16 project.
   - Place source audio as the master track at time zero.
   - Replace lyric-card visuals with generated shots; use exact verified lyric timing if typography is retained.
   - Use cuts and transitions that follow real musical events.
7. **Quality assurance**
   - Check opening, every scene boundary, every lyric-heavy section, climax, final audio tail.
   - Check aspect ratio, black frames, duplicate/frozen frames, visual artifacts, character continuity, and audio clipping/sync.
   - Confirm final duration and verify audio is the original source.
8. **Publish and archive**
   - Export the final video and a lightweight preview if storage allows.
   - Commit final storyboard, prompts, asset manifest, QA notes, and final media location.
   - Do not label anything FINAL until it has been exported and checked.
